# Research: Academic publications and benchmark papers for OSINT agents and their evaluation

## Summary

Direct OSINT-agent evaluation remains much thinner than the surrounding general-agent benchmark literature. The strongest directly relevant evaluation artifact found is **CyberThreat-Eval**, which releases expert-annotated CTI workflow data and evaluation code; the MDPI toolkit paper supplies a substantial but apparently unreleased 600-question evaluation, while the IEEE/TechRxiv framework and OpenReview course paper are primarily architecture/proof-of-concept contributions. **OSINTBench** is useful open-source harness code, but its repository explicitly requires users to create a dataset and lists high-quality human-verified datasets as future work, so its public leaderboard is not presently an immutable, independently replayable benchmark.

For an immutable offline ten-case narrative benchmark, the best-supported design combines CyberThreat-Eval's workflow realism and analyst-oriented rubric dimensions, GAIA/BrowseComp's concise auditable ground truth, and WebArena's outcome-based validation. None of the requested benchmarks alone evaluates complete, citation-bearing OSINT narratives over a small frozen corpus; that remains the literature gap the proposed benchmark can address.

## Scope and evidence policy

- **Direct OSINT evidence** means the system or benchmark explicitly evaluates OSINT/CTI acquisition, investigation, or intelligence production.
- **Contextual general-agent evidence** means the benchmark evaluates browsing, tool use, or interactive agency but not OSINT practice specifically.
- Dates and versions below are taken from primary records when accessible. A current page date generated by an HTML renderer was not treated as a publication date.
- “No release verified” means no code/data link was found in the inspected primary record; it does not prove that no private artifact exists.
- Stable links were opened or resolved during this research. Access failures are recorded separately.

## Findings

### Direct OSINT and CTI evidence

1. **Agentic and Generative AI for Open-Source Intelligence and Cyber Investigations: Taxonomy, Evaluation, Challenges, and Future Directions**

   - **Authors:** Eduardo Almeida Palmieri; Mohamed Chahine Ghanem; Dipo Dunsin; Zubair Baig; Ed de Quincey; Kim-Kwang Raymond Choo.
   - **Date/version/venue:** arXiv v1, submitted 3 July 2026 (manuscript states received 2 July 2026); preprint/systematic survey, no peer-reviewed venue verified.
   - **Identifier:** [arXiv:2607.03233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03233); [HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03233v1).
   - **Contribution:** Systematic review of 74 studies and an 11-category taxonomy spanning LLM foundations, agentic systems, RAG, knowledge graphs, evaluation, and risk. It identifies a “hallucination-validation gap,” uneven OSINT lifecycle coverage, and a lack of standardized cross-study evaluation.
   - **Evaluation evidence:** This is secondary evidence, not a new benchmark. The paper states that collection and analysis dominate the literature while verification, reporting, dissemination, and decision support are underexplored. It contrasts multiple-choice cyber knowledge results with CyberThreat-Eval's realistic workflow findings and reports that agentic OSINT systems were evaluated only under benign conditions in its corpus.
   - **Code/data:** No survey data repository was verified from the accessible paper. The authors explicitly acknowledge expert-curated corpus construction rather than a fully reproducible database export; the text even retains a placeholder requesting precise search dates, which weakens systematic-review replayability.
   - **Ten-case relevance:** High as a requirements source: score provenance, hallucination, adversarial robustness, lifecycle coverage, and human oversight. Low as a reusable evaluation artifact.
   - **Claim/support/confidence:** **Claim:** Published OSINT capability demonstrations have outpaced standardized, reproducible evaluation. **Support:** direct statement and corpus synthesis in the survey; independently consistent with the artifact checks below. **Confidence:** high for the surveyed corpus, medium for the entire field because corpus search was curated rather than fully replayable.

2. **A Framework for Embedding Generative and Agentic AI in Open Source Intelligence**

   - **Authors:** Eduardo Almeida Palmieri; Mohamed Chahine Ghanem; Viktor Sowinski-Mydlarz; Dipo Dunsin.
   - **Date/version/venue:** 2025 IEEE 7th International Conference on Blockchain Computing and Applications (BCCA), pp. 838–844; IEEE record indicates 14 October 2025. TechRxiv v1 and v2 exist, but exact version posting dates could not be recovered from the blocked TechRxiv pages.
   - **Identifiers:** [IEEE DOI 10.1109/BCCA66705.2025.11229637](https://doi.org/10.1109/BCCA66705.2025.11229637); [TechRxiv v1 PDF](https://www.techrxiv.org/doi/pdf/10.36227/techrxiv.175623135.54287545/v1); [TechRxiv v2 record](https://www.techrxiv.org/doi/abs/10.36227/techrxiv.175623135.54287545/v2).
   - **Contribution:** Proposes a modular agentic OSINT architecture combining autonomous planning/tool orchestration, RAG, generative analysis, ethical safeguards, and human review. The described tool ecosystem includes established OSINT and threat-intelligence platforms.
   - **Evaluation evidence:** The accessible text describes a proof-of-concept missing-person scenario and claims comparative gains in coverage, accuracy, and timeliness. However, the same paper frames extensive benchmarking, deployment against live feeds, and public code/sample-data release as future work. Exact sample size, complete rubric, raw outputs, uncertainty, and statistical analysis were not verified.
   - **Code/data:** No released implementation or dataset was verified. The paper says a version “will be prepared” for open-source release, which is not evidence of present availability.
   - **Ten-case relevance:** Useful architecture and ethical/HITL checklist, but weak precedent for scoring. Its future-work metrics (speed, coverage, entity/event accuracy, analyst workload) could supplement a ten-case narrative rubric only after operational definitions are fixed.
   - **Claim/support/confidence:** **Claim:** This is a proof-of-concept rather than a reusable benchmark. **Support:** interpretation of the paper's future-work language plus absence of a verified release. **Confidence:** medium because TechRxiv full text was access-blocked and the IEEE page exposed limited metadata.

3. **Empowering LLMs with Toolkits: An Open-Source Intelligence Acquisition Method**

   - **Authors:** Xinyang Yuan; Jiarong Wang; Haozhi Zhao; Tian Yan; Fazhi Qi.
   - **Date/version/venue:** Published 7 December 2024, *Future Internet* 16(12), article 461.
   - **Identifier:** [DOI 10.3390/fi16120461](https://doi.org/10.3390/fi16120461); [publisher article](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-5903/16/12/461).
   - **Contribution:** A ReAct-like thought/action/observation agent with scheduling, memory, and tools for CVE lookup, open-source-license information, WHOIS/domain queries, and Bing search. It addresses time-sensitive knowledge by querying tools and retaining useful intelligence.
   - **Evaluation evidence:** The paper constructs 600 questions, equally divided among CVE, license, domain-information, and “other” categories. It evaluates Qwen-7B/14B/72B chat models as direct-answer baselines and as agents, with ChatGPT-3.5 as another baseline. Metrics are response accuracy, tool utilization rate, tool-choice accuracy, and valid-parameter rate. Three OSINT-experienced annotators score helpfulness, informativeness, validity, and related criteria on a 1–5 scale; disagreements with standard deviation over 1 trigger discussion, and responses averaging over 4 count as accurate. The paper reports an 86% accuracy increase on the best-performing dataset/category, but that is a relative/category-specific result rather than proof of general OSINT reliability.
   - **Code/data:** No public code or test-set repository was verified from the accessible article record. The paper describes the test construction but abstract-level and page-level evidence do not establish an independently replayable artifact.
   - **Ten-case relevance:** Strong precedent for tool-call diagnostics and expert adjudication. Less suitable for long-form narratives because its unit of evaluation is predominantly short question answering, and its live tools undermine immutable replay unless their outputs are captured.
   - **Claim/support/confidence:** **Claim:** Tool access materially improves freshness-dependent OSINT QA in the authors' test, but released evidence does not support broad reproducibility. **Support:** direct experimental-method text; artifact conclusion is an inspected-record interpretation. **Confidence:** high on methods, medium on artifact absence.

4. **OSINT Agent for Oversea Marketing Support with Memory, Knowledge, Tool-Using and Self-reflection**

   - **Authors:** OpenReview search metadata identifies “Z. Shen”; a complete authoritative author list could not be recovered, so no expanded name is asserted.
   - **Date/version/venue:** THU 2024 Winter AML course submission; no archival conference/journal acceptance verified.
   - **Identifier:** [OpenReview rj9Gwe2pVe](https://openreview.net/forum?id=rj9Gwe2pVe); [PDF attachment](https://openreview.net/attachment?id=rj9Gwe2pVe&name=pdf).
   - **Contribution:** Proposes an overseas-marketing OSINT agent combining memory, domain knowledge, tool automation, multimodal inputs, RAG, and self-reflection to integrate structured and unstructured information.
   - **Evaluation evidence:** The accessible primary metadata provides an abstract-level capability description. No benchmark size, controlled baseline, complete scoring rubric, uncertainty, or independently inspectable results could be verified because attachment extraction failed.
   - **Code/data:** No release verified.
   - **Ten-case relevance:** Conceptually relevant to memory, reflection, and multi-source synthesis, but insufficient as evaluation evidence. It should not be cited as proving reliability or reproducibility.
   - **Claim/support/confidence:** **Claim:** This is a course project/architecture report with evaluation evidence unavailable in this research pass. **Support:** direct OpenReview venue metadata plus access failure. **Confidence:** high on venue classification, low on unobserved paper details.

5. **CyberThreat-Eval: Can Large Language Models Automate Real-World Threat Research?**

   - **Authors:** Xiangsen Chen; Xuan Feng; Shuo Chen; Matthieu Maitre; Sudipto Rakshit; Diana Duvieilh; Ashley Picone; Nan Tang.
   - **Date/version/venue:** TMLR 2025 journal reference; arXiv v1 submitted 10 March 2026 and marked accepted at TMLR.
   - **Identifiers:** [arXiv:2603.09452v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09452v1); [TMLR/OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=tiFtZHwr7O).
   - **Contribution:** Expert-annotated benchmark modeled on an industry CTI workflow: triage, deep search, and threat-intelligence drafting. It explicitly replaces quiz-style evaluation with analyst-centric tasks and measures factual/content quality plus time and token cost.
   - **Evaluation evidence:** 488 triage articles; 55 deep-search inputs; 1,310 IoCs; 1,565 TTP labels; 412 threat-actor and 412 root-cause generation articles. It evaluates GPT-4o, o3-mini, fine-tuned GPT-4o, and fine-tuned GPT-4o-mini at temperature 0.01/seed 42. Reported triage recall is 0.9091–1.0000 but precision only 0.2717–0.3982. TTP precision is 0.1771–0.3480 and recall 0.1414–0.2270. Narrative outputs are judged on relevance, factual accuracy, comprehensiveness, clarity, coherence, and attribution (1–5); the calibrated LLM judge reportedly exceeded 95% agreement with experts on roughly 100 examples after two refinement rounds. Root-cause narratives were stronger than nuanced TTP mapping and threat-actor analysis. The paper concludes full automation still requires human oversight.
   - **Code/data:** [GitHub code/data](https://github.com/xschen-beb/CyberThreat-Eval) under MIT and [Hugging Face dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/xse/CyberThreat-Eval). The repository contains stage-specific data and evaluators. Some narrative/deep-search scoring requires an OpenAI-compatible API, so replay is not fully model-independent.
   - **Ten-case relevance:** Highest direct relevance. Reuse its narrative dimensions, expert calibration, workflow stages, cost/latency tracking, and separate structured-extraction scores. For immutability, replace live related-URL search and API-dependent judging with a frozen source bundle, pinned grader or adjudication record, and stored traces.
   - **Claim/support/confidence:** **Claim:** Realistic CTI workflows expose weaknesses hidden by simpler knowledge tests. **Support:** direct benchmark results and released artifacts. **Confidence:** high.

6. **OSINTBench**

   - **Authors/maintainer:** GitHub organization/user `ccmdi`; no academic paper or complete scholarly author list was found.
   - **Date/version/venue:** Active software benchmark/leaderboard; no archival publication or stable released dataset version verified.
   - **Identifiers:** [project](https://osintbench.org/); [GitHub](https://github.com/ccmdi/osintbench).
   - **Contribution:** Harness and leaderboard for geolocation, identification, temporal reasoning, general analysis, and refusals, with search, EXIF, reverse-image search, webpage, Overpass, and Street View tools.
   - **Evaluation evidence:** The site publishes model scores and describes “basic” tasks, but the repository warns that most outputs are judge-model evaluated. Crucially, users “need to manually create a dataset,” and “High quality, human-verified datasets” plus “Release” remain unchecked roadmap items. Therefore the public scores cannot presently be independently replayed from repository contents alone.
   - **Code/data:** MIT-licensed harness code is present. Benchmark cases/answer corpus are not released in the repository inspected.
   - **Ten-case relevance:** The JSON schema (case, images, context, tasks, answer) is a useful implementation starting point, and the multimodal categories are relevant. It is not evidence that an immutable ten-case benchmark is already available. A frozen local dataset, version hash, documented judge, and human-verified answer/evidence packet would be necessary.
   - **Claim/support/confidence:** **Claim:** OSINTBench is currently an evaluation harness/leaderboard, not a self-contained reproducible benchmark release. **Support:** direct repository README. **Confidence:** high.

### Contextual general-agent and browsing benchmarks

7. **GAIA: a benchmark for General AI Assistants**

   - **Authors:** Grégoire Mialon; Clémentine Fourrier; Craig Swift; Thomas Wolf; Yann LeCun; Thomas Scialom.
   - **Date/version/venue:** arXiv v1, 21 November 2023; ICLR 2024 conference paper.
   - **Identifiers:** [arXiv:2311.12983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12983); [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/25ae35b5b1738d80f1f03a8713e405ec-Abstract-Conference.html); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=fibxvahvs3).
   - **Contribution/evidence:** 466 human-authored real-world questions requiring reasoning, browsing, coding, tool use, and multimodal handling. Answers are short and unambiguous, scored by normalized quasi-exact match. Human accuracy was 92% versus 15% for GPT-4 with plugins. It releases 166 annotated development questions and withholds 300 answers for a leaderboard.
   - **Code/data:** [Official Hugging Face benchmark](https://huggingface.co/gaia-benchmark) with scoring function and gated/held-out answer structure.
   - **Ten-case relevance:** Strong model for concise ground truth, source-of-truth annotation, difficulty levels, attachments, and automatic scoring. Weak match for narrative quality, citations, and immutable offline replay because many tasks depend on the changing web and held-out server evaluation.

8. **AgentBench: Evaluating LLMs as Agents**

   - **Authors:** Xiao Liu; Hao Yu; Hanchen Zhang; Yifan Xu; Xuanyu Lei; Hanyu Lai; Yu Gu; Hangliang Ding; Kaiwen Men; Kejuan Yang; Shudan Zhang; Xiang Deng; Aohan Zeng; Zhengxiao Du; Chenhui Zhang; Sheng Shen; Tianjun Zhang; Yu Su; Huan Sun; Minlie Huang; Yuxiao Dong; Jie Tang.
   - **Date/version/venue:** arXiv v1 7 August 2023; v3 4 October 2025; ICLR 2024.
   - **Identifier:** [arXiv:2308.03688](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03688); [GitHub](https://github.com/THUDM/AgentBench).
   - **Contribution/evidence:** Eight interactive environments spanning operating system, database, knowledge graph, digital card game, lateral thinking, household, web shopping, and web browsing. The paper evaluates 27 API and open-source models and attributes common failure to long-term reasoning, decision-making, and instruction following. It is broad agent evaluation, not OSINT or evidence-grounded report evaluation.
   - **Code/data:** Datasets, environments, and integrated evaluation package are released.
   - **Ten-case relevance:** Supports recording trajectories and separating environment/task types. Its heterogeneous aggregate score and reused interactive environments do not directly validate narrative OSINT claims.

9. **WebArena: A Realistic Web Environment for Building Autonomous Agents**

   - **Authors:** Shuyan Zhou; Frank F. Xu; Hao Zhu; Xuhui Zhou; Robert Lo; Abishek Sridhar; Xianyi Cheng; Tianyue Ou; Yonatan Bisk; Daniel Fried; Uri Alon; Graham Neubig.
   - **Date/version/venue:** arXiv v1 25 July 2023; v4 16 April 2024; conference venue not asserted here because it was not shown in the inspected primary arXiv record.
   - **Identifiers:** [arXiv:2307.13854v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13854v4); [project](https://webarena.dev/); [canonical repository](https://github.com/web-arena-x/webarena).
   - **Contribution/evidence:** Self-hosted, fully functional sites across e-commerce, forums, collaborative development, and content management, plus map/calculator/scratchpad and documentation. It has 812 long-horizon tasks and programmatic validators for functional outcomes. Best GPT-4 baseline achieved 14.41% end-to-end success versus 78.24% human performance.
   - **Code/data:** Code, data, Docker environment resources, validators, and demonstrations are public; the repository points to AgentLab/BrowserGym for an enhanced modern infrastructure.
   - **Ten-case relevance:** The strongest precedent for frozen/self-hosted state and outcome-based validation. Adapt its snapshot and validator principles, but narrative OSINT requires claim/evidence/provenance scoring beyond a binary website-state check.

10. **BrowseComp: A Simple Yet Challenging Benchmark for Browsing Agents**

   - **Authors:** Jason Wei; Zhiqing Sun; Spencer Papay; Scott McKinney; Jeffrey Han; Isa Fulford; Hyung Won Chung; Alex Tachard Passos; William Fedus; Amelia Glaese.
   - **Date/version/venue:** arXiv v1, 16 April 2025; no peer-reviewed venue verified.
   - **Identifiers:** [arXiv:2504.12516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12516); [official implementation](https://github.com/openai/simple-evals/blob/main/browsecomp_eval.py).
   - **Contribution/evidence:** 1,266 difficult, entangled web questions requiring persistent and creative search. The initial 1,287 were reduced by 21 after finding answer-format, ambiguity, or correctness problems. Short predictions are graded for semantic equivalence by a model judge. GPT-4o scored 0.6% without browsing and 1.9% with browsing; OpenAI Deep Research solved roughly half, and multi-run aggregation improved performance. The authors explicitly state that BrowseComp is not a real user-query distribution and does not evaluate long answers or ambiguity resolution.
   - **Code/data:** Reference implementation and encrypted/downloaded test data are in [OpenAI simple-evals](https://github.com/openai/simple-evals), MIT licensed. The repository was deprecated for new model results in July 2025 but retains BrowseComp, HealthBench, and SimpleQA reference implementations.
   - **Ten-case relevance:** Strong precedent for hard multi-hop retrieval, leakage controls, answer auditing, and publishing a reference evaluator. Poor direct fit for narrative quality: it deliberately reduces outputs to short answers and uses the live web.

11. **Simple Evals**

   - **Authorship/date/venue:** OpenAI-maintained software repository, not one standalone academic paper; public repository now carries a July 2025 deprecation notice.
   - **Identifier:** [openai/simple-evals](https://github.com/openai/simple-evals).
   - **Contribution/evidence:** Lightweight, transparent reference evaluators emphasizing zero-shot chain-of-thought settings. For this review, its source-supported relevance is the released BrowseComp grader/data-loading logic and stable reference implementations, not its broad table of model scores across unrelated benchmarks.
   - **Code/data:** MIT-licensed code. It will not receive new models/benchmarks, so pinning a commit is necessary for immutable use.
   - **Ten-case relevance:** Useful engineering pattern: a small evaluator interface, deterministic sampling seed, explicit grader prompt, per-sample records, aggregate metrics, and HTML traces. It does not supply OSINT cases or a narrative rubric.

## Synthesis for an immutable offline ten-case narrative benchmark

**Direct evidence:** CyberThreat-Eval supports scoring separate workflow stages and narratives on relevance, factual accuracy, comprehensiveness, clarity, coherence, and attribution, with expert calibration. The MDPI paper supports tool-choice/parameter-validity diagnostics and multi-annotator adjudication. The 2026 survey supports explicit hallucination, provenance, adversarial, and lifecycle coverage checks.

**Contextual evidence:** GAIA and BrowseComp support compact, auditable answers and careful item validation; WebArena supports frozen environments and outcome validators; AgentBench supports trajectory-level failure analysis; Simple Evals supplies a transparent evaluator pattern.

**Researcher inference:** A defensible ten-case benchmark should package every case as immutable local sources plus manifest hashes, question/intelligence requirement, gold atomic claims, acceptable citations, distractors/contradictions, and one expert-authored narrative. Score (1) atomic factual support, (2) citation entailment and source identity, (3) contradiction handling, (4) completeness against required claims, (5) calibrated uncertainty/refusal, (6) narrative coherence, and (7) cost/latency/tool trace. Report case-level results and confidence intervals or raw ten-case vectors; a single percentage on ten cases creates false precision.

**Researcher inference:** Automated claim matching should be supplemented by blinded human adjudication. LLM-as-judge can reduce cost, but CyberThreat-Eval's >95% agreement is domain- and rubric-specific and followed prompt calibration; it does not justify importing an unvalidated judge unchanged.

## Contradictions and disagreements

1. **No shared benchmark versus OSINTBench's existence.** The 2026 survey says no standardized, open, community-adopted cross-study OSINT benchmark exists. OSINTBench does exist as a named project, but its own repository omits the cases and asks users to create a dataset. These statements are reconcilable: a harness/leaderboard is not necessarily a released, community-adopted benchmark corpus.

2. **Knowledge-test optimism versus workflow realism.** The survey reports that multiple-choice CyberMetric results can make LLMs look superhuman, while CyberThreat-Eval finds low precision, weak TTP mapping, and insufficient complex analysis in realistic workflows. This is a substantive evaluation-design disagreement: success on cybersecurity knowledge questions is not equivalent to analyst workflow competence.

3. **Claimed framework gains versus future validation.** The IEEE/TechRxiv framework claims improved coverage, accuracy, and decision speed in a proof-of-concept, but extensive benchmarks and open release are described as future work. Without case counts, raw outputs, and released evaluators, the performance claim is weaker than CyberThreat-Eval's evidence.

4. **Open/live web realism versus stable replay.** GAIA and BrowseComp gain realism from open-web search but are vulnerable to page drift and disappearance. WebArena gains replayability by self-hosting but narrows ecological realism. An offline narrative benchmark deliberately chooses reproducibility over measuring current-web navigation.

5. **Automatic grading versus narrative validity.** GAIA's exact-match and WebArena's state validators are robust because outputs are constrained. BrowseComp uses a semantic-equivalence model judge. CyberThreat-Eval uses a calibrated multidimensional LLM judge for narratives. No evidence supports treating these mechanisms as interchangeable.

## Missing evidence and open questions

- Exact TechRxiv v1/v2 posting dates and a verified version-difference summary were unavailable because both PDF/full-text endpoints returned HTTP 403. The v1 and v2 URLs resolve in search, but version chronology beyond their existence is not asserted.
- IEEE Xplore did not expose a usable article page in extraction. Conference metadata was corroborated through the DOI and an author’s University of Liverpool research-output record, but raw experimental tables were not independently inspected.
- The OpenReview PDF for `rj9Gwe2pVe` could not be extracted. Complete authorship, experimental sample size, baselines, quantitative results, and artifacts remain unverified.
- No public code/test-set release was found for DOI `10.3390/fi16120461`; a direct explicit data-availability statement was not recoverable through the blocked page fetch.
- OSINTBench's public dataset, ground-truth provenance, human-estimate methodology, judge prompt/version, and run manifests were not present in the inspected repository.
- CyberThreat-Eval's proprietary fine-tuning corpus is not released; the benchmark data/evaluation code are public, but reproducing fine-tuned model training is therefore not fully possible.
- A ten-case benchmark needs evidence on inter-rater reliability, minimal detectable differences, robustness to grader/model changes, contamination, and whether ten cases span enough OSINT task diversity. None of the reviewed work validates ten cases as a statistically representative sample.
- No requested source demonstrates a complete frozen, offline, citation-bearing, multi-source OSINT narrative benchmark with adversarial evidence and stable deterministic scoring.

## Rejected or deprioritized sources

- **`osintbench.com` commercial/editorial tool-review site:** rejected as a name collision; unrelated to the `osintbench.org` LLM benchmark.
- **ResearchGate mirrors, paper-summary sites, alphaXiv, arXiv aggregators, and SEO paper indexes:** deprioritized in favor of arXiv, DOI publisher records, OpenReview, conference proceedings, official repositories, and author institutional records.
- **Third-party GAIA agent SDKs and unofficial leaderboard wrappers:** rejected for claims about the original benchmark; the paper, ICLR proceedings, and official Hugging Face record are authoritative.
- **PapersWithCode/Hugging Face paper summaries:** used only for discovery where applicable, not as final evidence when primary records were accessible.
- **The 2026 survey's characterization of cited primary studies:** retained as secondary synthesis but not substituted for direct artifact checks where the primary source was available.

## Consensus

- Evaluation should target realistic multi-step workflows, not only multiple-choice knowledge or isolated extraction.
- Tool use and browsing need separate diagnostics from final-answer correctness.
- Factual accuracy, provenance/attribution, completeness, and operational cost are recurring evaluation dimensions.
- Current agents remain unreliable on nuanced synthesis, TTP mapping, verification, and complex report production; human oversight is consistently warranted by the strongest direct evidence.
- Reusable artifacts materially strengthen claims. CyberThreat-Eval, WebArena, AgentBench, GAIA, and BrowseComp provide substantially more inspectable evaluation infrastructure than architecture-only OSINT papers.
- Stable replay and live-web realism are in tension. An immutable offline benchmark should explicitly state that it evaluates research/synthesis over a frozen evidence universe, not fresh-web discovery.
